## Design Pillars

### High Contrast

One major advantage of a UPA style is the high contrast between elements. Often, the UPA style involves a simple colour pallete with very contrasting hues in a similar shade. Sometimes, shade alone is involved. This would mean low information density, allowing players to more easily understand what is going on. UPA often also includes dark striking lines that draw a shape out of the background. This could be used as a way to contextualize where a players attention should be focused.

![Examples of UPA Stlye](assets/UPA-3986415336.jpg)

### Joyful/Flashy

Hidden role games require high social engagement to be enjoyable. This can often be motivated by the game itself being 'flashy'. For example, the hidden role game [Fakin It](https://www.jackboxgames.com/games/the-jackbox-party-pack-3/fakin-it) uses dynamic transitions and animations to help players flow through the social dilema of voting against their friends. Simply, by making it look fun players will in turn make it feel fun.

### Consistency

Since players will be provided with a lot of information to handle in short amounts of time, its important that players have the ability to dicsern said information. By providing a consistent visual and design language, players will be more effective and unlikely to blame their own failing the fault of the game. This means button placements on the mobile screen should remain consistent throughout different roles. However, changing visual layouts during game states would help in communicating the drastic change. 

## Who are the Players?

This game will likely be played by three demographics; Families, Young Adults, Work Environments. These demographics are tied by one common player type however, non-gamers. Players without any game sense. The main pillars are all thought in a way to make the game as accesible as possible. Players should be able to immediately understand the happenings, and should have very limited options presented to them.
